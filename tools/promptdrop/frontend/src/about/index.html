Methodology & Sources

Why "five drops" and "a bottle per email" are both real numbers — and what PromptDrop actually counts.

The model

Every estimate is energy × (WUE + EWIF):

  • Energy — watt-hours per task, as a low/typical/high band per task type (chat, long/reasoning, image, video). Published measurements disagree by roughly 10×, so a single number would be false precision.
  • WUE (Water Usage Effectiveness) — on-site datacenter cooling water, in liters per kWh. This is the number behind "five drops per prompt."
  • EWIF (Energy Water Intensity Factor) — the off-site water consumed generating the electricity (thermoelectric plant cooling, hydro evaporation). This is the number that turns drops into bottles. Most headlines silently include or exclude it — PromptDrop always shows both parts separately.

The low bound pairs efficient-datacenter WUE with a clean grid; the high bound pairs evaporative cooling in a hot climate with a water-intensive grid. That makes the range an honest envelope, not a statistical confidence interval. The typical column is the number to quote.

The constants

  • Chat prompt energy: 0.2–3 Wh (typical 0.34). Google's August 2025 disclosure put a median Gemini text prompt at 0.24 Wh / 0.26 mL; OpenAI's stated average is ≈0.34 Wh / 0.32 mL; older and reasoning-heavy estimates run to a few Wh.
  • Long / reasoning tasks: 1–15 Wh — long-context and chain-of-thought workloads measured well above chat medians.
  • Image generation: 0.5–8 Wh (typical 3), from the Hugging Face / Luccioni et al. measurements of diffusion models.
  • Video (~5 s clip): 20–950 Wh. The widest and least settled band — research-scale measurements reach 3.4 MJ per clip.
  • WUE: 0.12–1.8 L/kWh (typical 0.4). AWS reported a fleet WUE of 0.12 in 2025, Microsoft 0.30, industry average ≈0.84; evaporative cooling in hot, dry climates runs far higher.
  • EWIF: 0.5–4 L/kWh (typical 1.8), from NREL / Macknick et al. water-consumption intensities across US generation mixes, as applied by Li et al. (UC Riverside), "Making AI Less Thirsty."
  • Comparisons: direct-use figures (shower, flush, bottle) are utility-grade averages; coffee and beef are lifecycle "virtual water" figures from the Water Footprint Network — the fair comparison class for our combined estimate.

Deliberately out of scope

PromptDrop counts operational water only. It excludes water embodied in chip fabrication, datacenter construction, and model training, and it does not attempt jurisdiction-, provider-, or model-version-specific precision. Those details change the answer less than the on-site/off-site accounting choice does — and chasing them produces a false sense of exactness that the underlying disclosures can't support. When providers publish better numbers, the bands tighten; the constants are reviewed on the site's annual data refresh.
