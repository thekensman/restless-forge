Hologram GIFs vs Video: Which Format Is Right for You?

Published March 2026 · Updated October 2026 · By Kenneth Cross

HoloPath can save the same hologram two ways: as an animated GIF, which its own encoder writes, or as a video, which your browser records. They are not two wrappers around the same file. On a test subject at default settings (Classic preset, four-sided pyramid layout, 24 frames), the GIF came out at 8.3 MB and the video at 0.12 MB, roughly seventy times smaller. Where that difference comes from decides which one your display should get.

What the GIF encoder does

  • One palette for the whole animation. GIF allows 256 colours. HoloPath builds a single palette by sampling every frame in turn, so all frames share the same 256 colours. A single-hue hologram suits this well; the banding article covers the cases that do not.
  • No dithering. Each pixel is mapped to the nearest palette colour. Gradients that run out of shades show steps rather than a pattern of dots.
  • Every frame stored whole. The encoder compresses each frame on its own and never stores just the part that changed, so file size grows in step with frame count: 24 frames made 8.3 MB, and 48 frames made 16.7 MB.
  • Timing in hundredths of a second. GIF stores delays in units of 10 ms, with a minimum of 20 ms in HoloPath. The default 80 ms per frame is stored as 8, which gives a loop of just under two seconds at 24 frames.
  • It loops by itself. The file is marked to repeat forever, so any browser, gallery app or messaging app plays it on a loop without being told.

Noise is the setting that hurts a GIF most. Random grain cannot be compressed, and dropping Noise from 25% to 0 took the same test from 8.3 MB to 3.7 MB.

What the video export does

The video option hands the finished frames to your browser's built-in recorder. HoloPath asks for MP4 with H.264 first and falls back to WebM, so Chrome, Edge and Safari normally give you an MP4, and Firefox gives you WebM. The recorder runs at 2 megabits per second and keeps far more than 256 colours. Video codecs also store mostly the changes between frames, and a hologram is mostly black that never changes, which is why the file is so small.

Two things behave differently from a GIF:

  • It records in real time. The animation plays once into the recorder, so a two-second loop takes about two seconds to encode. Keep the tab in front while it runs: browsers slow down background tabs, which can stretch the timing.
  • It does not loop on its own. A video file plays once unless the player is set to repeat. On a phone under a pyramid, that means turning on repeat in your player before you walk away.

Which to use where

Where it will play Better choice Why
Phone under a pyramid Either; video if your player can repeat Video is smaller and keeps smooth gradients; a GIF loops with no setup
LED hologram fan Whatever the fan's app accepts Fans convert files in their own software; check its manual
Tablet in a Pepper's Ghost box Video Smooth colour on a large screen, with the player set to repeat
Messaging apps Video Size limits; many apps convert GIFs to video anyway
Email GIF Most email clients play GIFs inline and do not play video
A web page Either A GIF needs no player; a looping, muted video is far lighter

The format menu names the video format your browser will produce before you generate anything. If your browser has no recorder HoloPath can use, the video option is disabled and says so. GIF always works.
