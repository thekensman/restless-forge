Writing that comes out of building the tools. Each of these started as something I had to work out to make a tool behave correctly, and turned out to be worth explaining on its own — how a sand table's coordinate system actually works, what your salary looks like after every honest deduction, how a tattoo studio really arrives at a number.

They are grouped by the tool they came from. Nothing here is a summary of what other sites already say; if a piece exists it is because there was something specific to get right.

Why These Exist at All

Almost every article here started as a bug or a wrong answer. The sand table piece exists because early conversions came out looking like corduroy, and working out why meant understanding what a ball dragging through sand can and cannot represent. The tax comparison piece exists because two job offers with a $10,000 gap turned out to favour the lower one once the state lines were accounted for. In both cases the tool needed the answer before the article could be written.

That ordering matters, and it is the main thing separating these from the enormous volume of writing that exists purely to rank. A piece here is downstream of code that had to work. Where an article states a number — a needle-time rate, a travel-line penalty, a bracket threshold — that number is the one the tool uses, not a figure gathered to fill a paragraph.

The practical consequence is that there are not many of them, and they appear irregularly. A tool that raised no interesting questions gets no article, which is why some tools here have five and others have none. That is the intended state rather than a backlog.
